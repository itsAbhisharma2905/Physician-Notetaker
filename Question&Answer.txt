TASK 1 Questions & Answer
1. How would you handle ambiguous or missing medical data in the transcript?

ANS-In clinical conversations, medical data is often incomplete or implicit. To handle this,
    I use a confidence-based extraction approach and avoid hard assumptions. If a symptom, diagnosis, or treatment is not clearly mentioned,
    I mark it as “Not reported” rather than inferring it. I also apply context-aware and temporal analysis to distinguish between past and current symptoms,
    and flag low-confidence entities for human-in-the-loop validation, which is important for clinical safety.

2. What pre-trained NLP models would you use for medical summarization?

ANS-For medical summarization, I prefer domain-adapted transformer models. Models like ClinicalBERT and BioBERT are trained 
    on clinical notes and biomedical text, so they capture medical terminology better than generic models. For entity extraction,
    scispaCy works well for medical NER. In practice, I use a hybrid pipeline combining extractive summarization, transformer-based embeddings,
    and rule-based validation to ensure accuracy and reduce hallucination.

TASK 2 Questions & Answer
1. How would you fine-tune BERT for medical sentiment detection?

ANS-To fine-tune BERT for medical sentiment detection, I would start with a domain-adapted model like BioBERT or ClinicalBERT 
    instead of generic BERT. I’d fine-tune it on labeled patient utterances with sentiment classes such as Anxious, Neutral,
    and Reassured. During training, I’d use cross-entropy loss, handle class imbalance, and include context from previous dialogue 
    turns so the model understands sentiment progression in a clinical conversation. I’d evaluate performance using F1-score rather than accuracy,
    since emotional classes are often unevenly distributed.

2. What datasets would you use for training a healthcare-specific sentiment model?

ANS-I would use a mix of clinical and patient-generated datasets. For clinical language understanding, datasets like MIMIC-III/IV and i2b2/n2c2 
    are very useful. For emotional expression and concerns, patient health forums (such as MedHelp or HealthBoards) provide realistic sentiment 
    data. If needed, I’d also generate synthetic doctor–patient conversations to balance classes like reassurance or anxiety. This combination helps the model 
    capture both medical context and patient emotion accurately.

TASK 3 Questions & Answer

1. How would you train an NLP model to map medical transcripts into SOAP format?

ANS-To train an NLP model for SOAP note generation, I would use doctor–patient transcripts paired with clinician-written SOAP 
    notes as training data. Each sentence in the transcript would be labeled as Subjective, Objective, Assessment, or Plan.
    A transformer-based model like ClinicalBERT can then be fine-tuned to classify and group sentences into the correct SOAP sections.
    After classification, the extracted information is structured and summarized to generate a complete SOAP note.

2. What rule-based or deep-learning techniques would improve the accuracy of SOAP note generation?

ANS-Accuracy can be improved using a hybrid approach. Rule-based logic helps map patient statements to Subjective and physician examination 
    findings to Objective sections. Deep-learning models handle context and medical terminology, especially for Assessment and Plan. Adding 
    post-processing rules, temporal ordering, and confidence checks helps prevent hallucinations and keeps the SOAP notes clinically reliable.
